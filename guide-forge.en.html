FORGE — Compose a Skill

FORGE is where you build a skill: a working note / digital recipe you can save, reuse, and carry to any device. This guide walks you through it step by step in real detail — not just "what to click" but also why and how to know you succeeded.

01 First, understand: what is a "skill"?

In SKILL//ORBIT, a "skill" is a single text file called SKILL.md holding two parts:

  • Metadata (on top) — the "identity card": name, description, version, author, tags. Why it matters: this is what other apps / agents use to recognize and search for your skill.
  • Content / procedure (below) — the working recipe: when to use it, what's needed, how to run it step by step, and how to verify results are correct.

Why a plain file? Because simple text is portable (readable anywhere) and standard (not locked to one app). In FORGE you shape this file through an easy form, so you never need to memorize messy Markdown.

02 Open FORGE

1

Enter from the Home page

Open the app. On the landing page there are three big cards; click "FORGE — compose a skill", or just the FORGE tab in the top menu.

What you'll see: the page splits into two — left is TELUSUR / GALERI (browse & pick a source repo), right is the wide COMPOSE area where you fill in every field.

Important: remember the split. Left = pick & browse, right = your working/crafting area. Press ⛶ PREVIEW whenever you want to see the full SKILL.md file as a big pop-up.

SUCCESS CHECK You see the browse panel on the left and the COMPOSE form on the right.

03 Pick a starting point — 3 ways

2

Way A — from GALERI (easiest)

At the top-left there's a "Galeri" block with ready-to-use skill patterns (e.g. finance recap, content, procedures). Click a pattern, then press "Terapkan / Apply".

Why use this: the form fills with a clean structure as a starting point, so you only adjust it — fastest for beginners.

SUCCESS CHECK The COMPOSE form fills in automatically and you can start editing.

3

Way B — from a GitHub repo (use "Racik")

In the "Telusur GitHub" block, type a keyword (e.g. planning or proposal) and press Search. On a result you like, press "Racik".

What happens behind the scenes: the app pulls that public skill's content and turns it into an editable draft. A repo with no SKILL.md still won't stop you — it builds a draft from the README. Never dead-ends. A panel "THE SKILL'S CONTENT (from repo)" shows the original before you Racik.

Heads up: a draft from a list-type repo (collection of links, not one skill) is usually just a general skeleton — tags reset to empty, so fill in your own tags and write the recipe so it's truly useful.

SUCCESS CHECK The repo name is shown as the source, the form carries the draft, and you can start editing.

4

Way C — from scratch (manual)

Clear all fields and type them yourself: name, description (short, ≤60 chars), tags, author, and version.

When it fits: when you already know exactly the recipe you want to write and need no example.

04 Write the skill content (the most important part)

5

Fill the "content / how it works" field with a clear sequence of steps

Write instructions as a simple order: start with what → do what → when is it done / how to know it's right. Imagine explaining to someone who has never done it.

Recommended writing style:
  • Use short command sentences ("Open…", "Fill…", "Record…").
  • Break into at most 3–5 steps. One step = one action.
  • At the end, write a completion criterion: what result proves your step was right.

Tip: a good skill is sharp — focus on one main job, not ten at once. If it gets too long, split it into several skills.

6

Choose "behaviors" & "tools" (toggles)

Below the content field are behavior (how many steps, whether it ends with a check) and tool (terminal / API / file) options describing how the skill is used. Click to toggle them on/off.

Why: this metadata helps other apps decide when and how to use your skill correctly.

05 Check the PREVIEW

7

Press the PREVIEW button to see your file

Press ⛶ PRATINJAU / PREVIEW and a wide pop-up opens showing the full SKILL.md about to be saved. Read top to bottom: is the metadata tidy? Does the content cover every step you meant?

Note: the preview is view-only. If anything is off, don't edit it there — close the pop-up and change it in the COMPOSE area; the preview follows.

Extra: your fields are autosaved on this device as you type, and you can also Import a .SKILL.md file to load it straight into the form.

SUCCESS CHECK The preview pop-up reflects all your input and looks tidy with nothing awkward.

06 SAVE

8

Save to your collection & (optional) cloud

Press "SIMPAN / SAVE" in the preview pop-up (or the save button in the compose area). The skill goes to ARSIP (your collection on this device). If you're signed in online, you can also sync it to the server for other devices.

Other options: Copy (file to clipboard) and Download (save as a .SKILL.md file) — handy for sharing or storing outside the app.

SUCCESS CHECK A confirmation appears and the skill shows in the ARSIP list.

07 The fastest way (shortcut)

  1. Open FORGE.
  2. In Galeri, click the pattern closest to your need → Apply.
  3. Change name & description, type your steps in the content field.
  4. Look at Preview → Save.

Result: usually under 2 minutes for your first skill.

08 Behind the scenes

  • Racik calls raw.githubusercontent.com, tries to read SKILL.md, and falls back to README.md when missing — that's why it never comes back empty.
  • The Galeri panel holds built-in patterns; Radar pulls real star counts from the GitHub API.
  • Saving to ARSIP writes to this device's local storage; saving to cloud (after login) sends to the SKILL//ORBIT server through a secured API.

09 Real use cases

USE CASE 1 · Small-business bookkeeping

A business owner in Sekadau wants a weekly stall finance recap without the hassle.

  • Pick GALERI → the "rekap-keuangan" pattern → Apply.
  • Rename it to rekap-keuangan-warung, description "Auto bookkeeping & P&L for a stall."
  • Write steps: record nightly turnover → fill fixed columns → compute the difference → save the summary.
  • Preview → tidy → Save.

USE CASE 2 · WiFi team procedure

Make a WiFi installation checklist so the team stays consistent for every customer.

  • From scratch: name = checklist-instalasi-wifi.
  • Steps: survey location → mount AP → configure → test signal → record customer data.
  • Finish with a completion criterion (stable signal at every point).
  • Save; can be shared with the team as a standard reference.

USE CASE 3 · Daily content pack

Generate 5 content ideas from one topic every week.

  • Use "Racik" on a popular public content repo (see RADAR).
  • Turn its content into a prompt: "Make 5 content ideas about topic X."
  • Save as a reusable skill.

10 Frequently asked

Why are my tags empty after Racik?

Because the repo didn't supply tags, the app clears them (instead of borrowing a stray example tag). Type your own tags, separated by commas.

The preview can't be edited, right?

Correct. Preview is just a view. Change everything in the COMPOSE area; the preview follows automatically. It's designed so the final file stays tidy.

What's the difference between SAVE, Copy, and Download?

SAVE → into ARSIP (the in-app collection). Copy → copies the file to the clipboard to paste anywhere. Download → saves a .SKILL.md file on your device.

Will my skills be lost without an account?

No — as long as they're saved to ARSIP on that device, the local collection is safe. The online account is only for backup & moving between devices.

Can skill names use capital letters and spaces?

Better to use lowercase, no spaces, with - separators (e.g. rekap-keuangan-warung). Spaces/special characters are more likely to clash.

11 Glossary

SKILL.md — the composed text file, holding metadata + procedure.

Metadata — the "identity card" at the top: name, description, version, author, tags.

Galeri — the collection of ready-to-use skill patterns on the left panel.

Racik — the action that pulls a public skill from GitHub into an editable draft.

Preview — the pop-up that shows the final file as it's being formed.

ARSIP — your collection of every skill you've saved.

Tags — category labels for easier searching (comma-separated).
